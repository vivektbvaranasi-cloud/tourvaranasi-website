Saryu Aarti in Ayodhya: An Evening at Ram ki Paidi and Naya Ghat

Where to go for Saryu Aarti in Ayodhya, when to reach the ghats, how Deep Daan fits into the evening and what to ask before arranging a boat or a chair.

From our Ayodhya operations team
Last locally checked: October 2026

Ayodhya access changes with security, crowd levels and religious dates. Our practical guidance is based on current guest operations, including realistic vehicle stopping points, walking or e-rickshaw movement and the time needed for darshan.

Written & reviewed by Tour Varanasi Operations Team · How we verify our travel guides

Published 20 November 2026 · Tour Varanasi Operations Team

For the evening Saryu Aarti, head towards the Naya Ghat riverfront beside Ram ki Paidi. Ask your host for that evening’s start time and a specific meeting point. Aim to reach the riverfront about 45 minutes beforehand. This is time to walk in and settle, especially if several generations are travelling together.

Where should the driver drop you?

Ram ki Paidi is a series of ghats, so giving everyone the name alone can leave members of the same party on different stretches of the riverfront. Ask your guide to send a pin or name a visible landmark for both the inward walk and the return pickup. Parking and vehicle barriers can shift on busy evenings. If travelling independently, agree where to find your driver before leaving the car.

If you arrive early, walk part of the Paidi and visit nearby Nageshwarnath Temple. Its association with Kush, son of Rama, is part of local tradition. Stop the walk in time to reach the aarti area without hurrying. The district’s pages show the Ram ki Paidi riverfront and describe Nageshwarnath Temple.

What time is Saryu Aarti?

Plan around dusk. The ceremony time can change with the season or a festival programme, so use the time confirmed in Ayodhya on the day. Work backwards from it: first allow your settling-in time at the ghat, then the walk from the drop-off, then the drive from the hotel. If a delayed arrival leaves time for just one thing, go straight to the ceremony and save the Paidi walk for morning.

Choose a place that works for the whole family

You can watch from the public ghat area. For a traveller who finds standing difficult, ask in advance whether a chair is available, where it will be placed and how many steps lie between it and the drop-off. A front seat only helps if the person can reach it comfortably. A spot farther back with easier access may make a better evening. If you bring a wheelchair, agree on a reachable viewing point and check the return route before the crowd gathers.

Deep Daan and an optional boat ride

Deep Daan is the offering of a lit diya. Tell your guide if your family would like to offer one so you can choose a permitted place and follow the attendants’ directions. You can also stay in your viewing spot and take part in the prayer there.

If you would like a boat ride as well, ask for a daylight outing followed by a return to shore for aarti. The operator can confirm the boarding point, the day’s last departure and river conditions. On a winter visit we suggest asking about a 4 pm outing first. Allow time to disembark and reach the viewing area. A delayed arrival is a good reason to drop the boat and keep the ceremony.

On Deepotsav and other large festival evenings, pedestrian access and viewing arrangements may follow a special plan. Confirm arrangements for the particular date before reserving a chair, boat or transfer. For an ordinary overnight stay, the one-night Ayodhya itinerary pairs this evening with Ram Mandir the following morning.

Arrange your evening at the Saryu

Share your date, hotel and group size. Mention chairs, walking assistance, Deep Daan or a daytime boat so we can check each arrangement for your visit.
