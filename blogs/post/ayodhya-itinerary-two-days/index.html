Ayodhya in Two Days: A One-Night Itinerary for Ram Mandir and the Saryu

Arriving in Ayodhya for one night? Spend the first evening by the Saryu and the next morning at Ram Mandir, with time for Hanuman Garhi or Kanak Bhawan.

From our Ayodhya operations team
Last locally checked: October 2026

Ayodhya access changes with security, crowd levels and religious dates. Our practical guidance is based on current guest operations, including realistic vehicle stopping points, walking or e-rickshaw movement and the time needed for darshan.

One night in Ayodhya works well if you arrive by mid-afternoon and leave after lunch the next day. Spend the evening beside the Saryu, sleep in Ayodhya, and give Ram Lalla darshan your first full morning. If your return train leaves early, move darshan to the arrival day and treat the river visit as optional.

Day one — arrive, walk Ram ki Paidi and stay for Saryu Aarti

Go to your hotel first. Leave your luggage, have lunch and arrange a pickup point for the evening. Old Ayodhya is easier to explore with a small bag, and your car may have to stop well before the temple lanes or the ghats. For a mixed-age family, agreeing on the return meeting point now saves a search for the driver after dark.

Start at Ram ki Paidi while the steps and the shapes of the ghats are still visible. Along this stretch, Nageshwarnath gives the afternoon a different focus: it is a Shiva shrine in a city best known for Rama. Local tradition associates its foundation with Kush, Rama’s son. Leave time to return to the river before the evening aarti. Our Saryu Aarti guide explains where to meet, how to choose a viewing spot and how to fit in Deep Daan.

Day two — Ram Mandir, then the temple lanes

Build the morning around your darshan arrangements. Before leaving the hotel, confirm the approach in use that day and where to leave phones and belongings. Allow time for the walk from the vehicle point, security and the queue. If family members move at different speeds, agree where to regroup after collecting your things. Keep lunch flexible until everyone is back together.

Hanuman Garhi and Kanak Bhawan are two different outings even though they share the temple quarter. At Hanuman Garhi, the approach to the shrine involves a flight of steps. Some families prefer to pay their respects to Hanuman first; if this matters to yours, reverse the morning order and allow for both queues. At Kanak Bhawan, pause in the courtyard before visiting the shrine dedicated to Rama and Sita. If you have an afternoon train, choose the stop that matters more and keep a margin for the journey to the station.

When is a second night worthwhile?

If your first day begins with a late train or a long road transfer, two nights give you a full day between journeys. They also suit travellers who want a midday rest, have difficulty with steps, or want an outing to Guptar Ghat. If someone uses a wheelchair, ask about the transfer from the hotel, the temple approach and the ghat separately. Our Ram Mandir wheelchair guide explains how temple assistance and a wheelchair for the whole day differ.
