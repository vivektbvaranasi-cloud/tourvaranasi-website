Lucknow in Two Days: A First-Time Itinerary

Spend two days in Lucknow with a morning at the Imambaras, an evening in Chowk and time at the Residency. See how to adapt the route around your arrival.

From our Lucknow operations team
Last locally checked: October 2026

Our Lucknow planning is built around realistic monument timings, city traffic, guide coordination and the pace that works for private travellers rather than a rushed sightseeing checklist.

With two days in Lucknow, spend the first in the old city around the Imambaras and Chowk. Use the second for the Residency and Hazratganj. This leaves time for both the great vaulted hall at Bara Imambara and a quieter pleasure, sitting down to a fresh Lucknawi lunch before the afternoon gets away from you.

This plan assumes two sightseeing mornings and at least one evening. If you arrive after lunch and leave the following morning, use the one-day Lucknow guide. A single night works for this two-day plan if your first train arrives early and your onward train leaves late the next day.

Day one in the old city

Morning. Begin in the courtyards of Bara Imambara before visiting the main hall. Nawab Asaf-ud-Daula commissioned the complex during a famine in the 1780s. Stand near one end of the hall and look along the length of the vaulted ceiling. From here a guide can explain how the structure relates to the maze of passages above. The Bhul Bhulaiya involves stairs. If part of the family would prefer to stay below, agree on a meeting place before anyone starts climbing.

Continue past Rumi Darwaza towards Chhota Imambara. Seen together, the two Imambaras give very different impressions of the city. Allow time to look at Chhota Imambara’s ornament and chandeliers after the scale of the great hall. These are religious sites. Ask what is open to visitors during prayers or observances and where photography is welcome. The district’s Bara Imambara guide gives the historical background.

Afternoon and evening. Keep lunch close to the old city or return to your hotel for a break. Later, meet your guide near a named entrance to Chowk. One embroidery stop and one or two tastings make a comfortable evening. Pick a Chowk food and chikankari route to suit the group. If everyone has had enough walking, a seated Awadhi dinner is a good way to finish.

Day two at the Residency and Hazratganj

Morning. Visit the Residency while you have time to read the signs and walk through the grounds. Start with the site plan and follow the story of the 1857 siege through a small selection of buildings. The roofless ruins take a little explanation. A guide who can connect the buildings to the people who lived and worked here adds more than another sightseeing stop. The district guide to the Residency is useful background before you go.

Afternoon. Have lunch around Hazratganj. Choose a short shopping stop if you want to compare a few garments in one place. For an interest in how chikankari is made, arrange to meet a maker in advance and leave the afternoon for that visit. A demonstration takes time, and the workshop may be in a different neighbourhood from the shop. Ask for the address when you book.

When a third day is worth it

If the first flight lands late, shift the monuments to your first full morning and use arrival evening for dinner. Add a night if you want the Residency, a textile demonstration and a leisurely visit with a local host. Tea or dinner with Nawab Saheb is an optional arrangement, subject to the host’s availability. If your next stop is Ayodhya, keep the transfer on a separate morning when a full day of temple visits matters to your family.

For car and guide arrangements, see the private two-day Lucknow tour. If your dates allow longer, compare the three-day programme and the Lucknow tour prices and inclusions.
