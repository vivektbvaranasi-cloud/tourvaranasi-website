Ganga Aarti in Varanasi: The Best Way to Experience It

Balcony viewing, Sankalp participation, reserved chairs or a boat all feel very different. We recommend the option that gives you the best experience, not simply the most famous photograph.

Quick answer

For most of our guests, we prefer balcony viewing for the clearest overall view or Sankalp Aarti participation for a closer, more personal religious experience. Reserved chairs are useful for comfort and mobility. A boat gives a broad riverfront perspective but is often farther from the priests, may be blocked by other boats and does not always carry the chanting and atmosphere as strongly.

What is Aarti?

Aarti is a Hindu ritual in which light is offered during prayer. At the riverfront, priests use lamps, incense, bells and chanting in a public ceremony dedicated to the Ganges. It is a working religious ritual rather than a stage performance created for visitors.

Our first choice: balcony viewing

When a good balcony position is available, this is often the most balanced way to watch the ceremony. You are elevated above the crowd, the full formation of priests is visible and your line of sight is less likely to be interrupted. It is especially useful for first-time visitors who want to understand the scale and choreography of the Aarti without standing in a dense crowd.

Sankalp Aarti: for guests who want to participate

Sankalp Aarti is different from simply watching. Guests sit closer to the ritual and participate with guidance, making it a more personal spiritual experience. We particularly recommend it for travellers who are interested in the meaning of the ceremony and want to feel involved rather than treating Aarti as sightseeing.

Reserved chair seating

Reserved land-based chair seating is a practical option for senior travellers, families and guests who prefer a stable position without prolonged standing. It can also be a good alternative when a balcony is not available. The exact position varies, so we confirm the arrangement for the travel date rather than promising a generic “front row”.

Why we do not automatically recommend boat viewing

Watching from a boat can be atmospheric because you see the illuminated ghats from the Ganges. However, during Aarti the boats gather in rows. Your boat may end up farther from the priests than expected and another boat can partially obstruct the view. The chanting, bells and collective atmosphere also feel less immediate from the river. We therefore use boat viewing when the guest specifically prefers the river perspective, not as our automatic premium recommendation.

Getting there: expect a walking section

For the main Dashashwamedh Ghat area, the vehicle normally cannot drive beside the ceremony. Guests are generally dropped around Benia Parking or another practical point depending on current traffic restrictions, then continue on foot with the guide through the pedestrian area. We agree the return meeting point with the driver before the Aarti because thousands of people may leave at the same time.

If you are staying at BrijRama, Guleria Kothi or Taj Ganges Avantika

These hotels use their hotel jetty at Namo Ghat for arrival and departure. Their hotel-operated boat transfers between Namo Ghat and the property are complimentary and included in the hotel booking package. This is separate from an optional Aarti-viewing boat and should not be charged as an additional arrival/departure boat service.

Arrive with time to spare

The central ghat area becomes busy well before the ceremony starts. A private guide manages the walking approach, viewing location and return movement. On weekends, festival dates and peak periods we leave more buffer because crowd-control barricades can alter the normal route.

Aarti timing changes with the season

Do not build a flight, train or tight dinner reservation around one fixed Aarti time copied from the internet. The ceremony timing varies with the season and special dates can change crowd-control arrangements. We reconfirm close to travel and keep enough time for the walk back to the vehicle.

Photography and etiquette

Photography and video are common, but we encourage guests to put the phone away for part of the ritual. During Sankalp participation especially, the experience is better when the focus is on the ceremony rather than continuous filming. Your guide can explain the sequence before it starts so a running commentary is not necessary.

Pair it with the old city

For a first visit, we like to approach the Aarti after a guided old-city or bazaar walk. The evening then becomes the natural conclusion to a journey through the living religious city rather than an isolated spectacle.
