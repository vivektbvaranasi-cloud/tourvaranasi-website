First Time in Varanasi

A local guide to planning your first visit — how long to stay, where to base yourself, what to see and how to make the city more comfortable.

Quick answer

For most first-time international visitors, we recommend two or three days. The city is easier when sunrise, temples, Sarnath and the evening river ceremony are spread across separate outings rather than packed into one long day.

How many days do you need in Varanasi?

One day works when time is limited, but it is intensive. Our 1 Day Varanasi Tour is designed as a full private day.

Two days is a good minimum for many first-time visitors. It gives time for the old city and Ganga Aarti, then sunrise, sacred Varanasi and Sarnath. See our 2 Day Varanasi Tour.

Three days is our preferred pace for travellers who want proper breaks and a little more cultural depth. See the 3 Day Varanasi Tour.

Four days works especially well for travellers who dislike rushed sightseeing. It allows shorter outings, more hotel time and optional food, music or craft experiences. See the 4 Day Varanasi Tour.

Where should you stay?

Riverfront and ghat hotels give you atmosphere and immediate access to the old city, but vehicle access can be limited. Some transfers may involve walking or, for particular properties, boat arrangements.

Assi suits travellers who want river life in a slightly more open part of the city.

Cantonment and Nadesar are usually easier for private cars and suit guests who value quiet rooms, larger hotel facilities and straightforward road access.

The best hotel area depends on what matters most to you: river view, easy vehicle access, a lift, larger rooms, heritage character or quiet nights.

What should a first-time visitor definitely see?

We would normally build a first visit around five things: sunrise on the Ganges, the evening Ganga Aarti, an old-city walk, sacred Varanasi including Kashi Vishwanath where appropriate, and Sarnath.

Beyond these, food, weaving, music and neighbourhood experiences can be added according to your interests rather than simply filling the day with more sightseeing.

Is Varanasi difficult to explore?

Parts of it can be. Old Varanasi has narrow lanes, pedestrian areas, crowds, uneven surfaces and changing vehicle restrictions. That is part of the character of the city, but it needs sensible planning.

A private guide and driver make the practical side easier. We use the car where it is useful, walk where cars cannot go and adjust the route when local access changes.

How much walking is involved?

Most visitors should expect some walking. The old city cannot be experienced entirely from a car. The amount can, however, be reduced by choosing shorter routes, using suitable drop-off points and avoiding unnecessary backtracking.

If mobility is a concern, tell us before travel. We can plan a gentler version rather than discovering the problem after you arrive.

Is Varanasi suitable for travellers aged 60 or 70+?

Yes, for many travellers, provided the programme is planned around comfort rather than speed. Hotel location, shorter walking routes, rest time, private transport and comfortable Ganga Aarti viewing can make a substantial difference.

We do not describe Varanasi as fully accessible because many old lanes, ghats and temple approaches are not. Instead, we explain the practical limitations and adjust the programme honestly.

Sunrise boat ride

Morning on the Ganges is one of the best introductions to Varanasi. From the river you see bathing rituals, temple activity, residents exercising, laundry, boats and the long line of ghats in softer light.

We normally prefer a private boat. Operations remain subject to river levels and local administration. Read our Sunrise Boat Ride in Varanasi guide.

Ganga Aarti: seating or boat?

The evening Aarti is a large public ceremony. Reserved seating is often the more comfortable choice for mature travellers because you have a defined place to sit. A boat can give a wider view when river operations are permitted.

There is no single best option for everyone. We advise according to the season, crowd levels and your comfort. See our Ganga Aarti experience.

Kashi Vishwanath Temple

Kashi Vishwanath is one of the city’s most important temples. Darshan simply means seeing or being in the presence of the deity.

Security procedures are strict and access arrangements can change. Phones and other items may need to be left in lockers, and walking is normally required from the nearest permitted access point. We confirm the practical procedure close to the visit rather than making a permanent promise online.

Manikarnika Ghat

Manikarnika is one of Varanasi’s principal cremation ghats. Cremation here is part of living religious practice, not a staged visitor attraction.

A knowledgeable guide can explain the beliefs and customs respectfully. Intrusive photography of cremations should be avoided.

Sarnath

Sarnath is important for both history and Buddhism. It is where the Buddha gave his first sermon after enlightenment. Dhamek Stupa, the archaeological remains and the Buddhist institutions around the site give the visit a very different atmosphere from central Varanasi.

We normally allow a separate half day rather than rushing it between other sights.

Best time to visit

The cooler months are the easiest for long days outdoors. Summer can be very hot, while the monsoon can affect river levels and boat operations. Festival dates can be memorable but also much busier.

There is no need for a complicated weather table: choose your dates around your tolerance for heat, crowds and early starts, then allow us to adjust the programme to the season.

What should you wear?

Comfortable, modest clothing works best. Shoulders and knees should generally be covered for religious visits. Shoes are removed at many temples, so easy footwear is useful.

You do not need to dress like a pilgrim. Your guide will explain local customs as they arise.

What should visitors avoid?

Avoid trying to photograph every ritual, following unofficial access promises, overloading each day and treating cremation areas as spectacle. Also avoid assuming a car can take you directly to every temple or ghat.

Varanasi is much easier when you leave a little space in the day.
