Itinerary

Your day-by-day itinerary

DAY 01

Arrive Varanasi — Old City & Ganga Aarti

After arrival, settle into your hotel before meeting your private guide. Begin with a measured walk through the Northern Bazaar and selected old-city lanes, allowing Varanasi to reveal itself gradually rather than rushing between sights. Continue towards the river for the evening Ganga Aarti, with a pre-arranged viewing position selected according to crowd levels, local restrictions and your preferred degree of comfort.

Sightseeing: Northern Bazaar, old-city lanes, Dashashwamedh Ghat and evening Ganga Aarti

Overnight: Varanasi

DAY 02

Sunrise, Sacred Varanasi & Sarnath

Begin before sunrise with a private boat on the Ganges and a short walk along the ghats. Return to the hotel for breakfast and a break, then continue with selected sacred and cultural sights in the city. In the afternoon travel to Sarnath to understand the Buddhist story of Varanasi through the main archaeological remains, stupas and Mulagandha Kuti Vihara.

Sightseeing: Private sunrise boat, Kashi Vishwanath where appropriate, Sankat Mochan, Tulsi Manas, BHU & New Vishwanath Temple, Bharat Mata Temple, Dhamek Stupa and Mulagandha Kuti Vihara

Overnight: Varanasi

DAY 03

Varanasi → Ayodhya — Riverfront Introduction

After breakfast, drive privately to Ayodhya and check into your hotel. Allow time to rest before beginning with the more open riverfront side of the city rather than entering the busiest temple area immediately. Walk around Ram Ki Paidi, visit Nageshwarnath Temple and continue to the Saryu riverfront for the evening Aarti, with local transport used where vehicle restrictions make it more practical.

Sightseeing: Nageshwarnath Temple, Ram Ki Paidi, Saryu riverfront and evening Saryu Aarti

Overnight: Ayodhya

DAY 04

Ram Janmabhoomi & Old Ayodhya

Start relatively early for the main temple area, where walking and local e-rickshaw movement are usually part of the experience because private vehicles cannot reach every entrance. Visit Shri Ram Janmabhoomi Mandir, Hanuman Garhi, Kanak Bhawan and Dashrath Mahal with your guide coordinating the sequence around crowd and security conditions. Later, keep time for the older lanes or Guptar Ghat if your energy and interests allow.

Sightseeing: Shri Ram Janmabhoomi Mandir, Hanuman Garhi, Kanak Bhawan, Dashrath Mahal and optional Guptar Ghat

Overnight: Ayodhya

DAY 05

Departure / Optional Nandigram

Enjoy a relaxed breakfast before your private transfer to Ayodhya Airport or the railway station. If you have a later departure, the morning can be used for an excursion to Nandigram and Bharat Kund, associated with Bharat during Lord Rama’s exile. The visit is added only when the onward timing allows it comfortably, rather than creating a rushed final morning.

Sightseeing: Optional Nandigram and Bharat Kund, subject to departure time

Overnight: Departure from Ayodhya

Good to know

Practical questions

Questions travellers often ask

How much walking is required around Ram Mandir?

Some walking is normally unavoidable because vehicles cannot reach every point in the main temple precinct. Local e-rickshaw or rickshaw movement may also be used. In practice, the amount of walking depends heavily on that day’s security cordons and where vehicles are stopped, so we never promise a fixed door-to-door drop. If you tell us about knee, back or mobility concerns in advance, we can reduce optional stops, allow more rest time and use approved local transport wherever it is actually permitted.

Can vehicles reach the temple entrance in Ayodhya?

No. Vehicle restrictions and security zones mean guests usually continue part of the way on foot or by approved local transport. In reality, the final vehicle stopping point changes with crowd control, security and the date of visit, so guests should expect part of the approach to be on foot or by approved local transport. We plan the drop, guide meeting point and return pickup before the visit so you are not left trying to find the vehicle in a restricted zone.

Can the itinerary be adjusted for mature travellers?

Yes. We can reduce walking, plan rest breaks and sequence temple visits around the guest’s comfort and current access conditions. In practice, the amount of walking depends heavily on that day’s security cordons and where vehicles are stopped, so we never promise a fixed door-to-door drop. If you tell us about knee, back or mobility concerns in advance, we can reduce optional stops, allow more rest time and use approved local transport wherever it is actually permitted.

How long does Ram Mandir darshan take?

Waiting times vary widely by date, security arrangements and crowd levels. We avoid promising a fixed duration. On ordinary days the process can move quite smoothly, but weekends, festival periods and security changes can make the same visit take much longer. We therefore keep the surrounding programme flexible and avoid scheduling a tight onward departure immediately after darshan. The local team reconfirms current access and guides the guest to the practical entry point, but the final waiting time remains with temple and security authorities.

Why stay two nights in Ayodhya?

Two nights allow time for Ram Mandir, the old city, Ram Ki Paidi and Sarayu Aarti without turning the visit into a single temple stop. We use nights to make the sightseeing work comfortably, not simply to increase the length of the package. An extra night often means the difference between seeing a place properly and spending most of the day in traffic or moving luggage.

Can special access always be guaranteed?

No. Any priority or special-access arrangement remains subject to temple, district and security regulations. Access procedures can change at short notice because the temple and security authorities control them, not private tour operators. We only offer an arrangement when it is genuinely available for the guest’s date and profile, and we do not sell a promise that cannot be honoured locally. If priority access is unavailable, we plan the normal darshan with realistic walking and waiting time instead.
