Itinerary

Your day-by-day itinerary

DAY 01

Arrive Varanasi — Old City & Ganga Aarti

After arrival, settle into your hotel before a private guided introduction to Banaras. Walk through the Northern Bazaar and selected old-city lanes towards the river, with time to understand the daily life, small shrines and local traditions that make the area more than a sightseeing route. End with the evening Ganga Aarti from a pre-arranged viewing position.

Sightseeing: Northern Bazaar, old-city lanes, Dashashwamedh Ghat and evening Ganga Aarti

Overnight: Varanasi

DAY 02

Sunrise Varanasi & Sarnath

Begin before sunrise with a private boat along the ghats, followed by a short walk and a return to the hotel for breakfast and rest. Later visit selected sacred and cultural Varanasi, then spend the afternoon at Sarnath. The programme gives the Buddhist site proper time for explanation while still keeping the overall day comfortable.

Sightseeing: Private sunrise boat, Kashi Vishwanath where appropriate, Sankat Mochan, BHU & New Vishwanath Temple, Bharat Mata Temple, Dhamek Stupa and Mulagandha Kuti Vihara

Overnight: Varanasi

DAY 03

Varanasi → Prayagraj

Drive privately to Prayagraj and begin with the Triveni Sangam, reached by local boat when river and local conditions allow. Continue with selected city sights rather than trying to cover everything in one afternoon. The exact order is adjusted to traffic, opening hours and your pace, with the hotel used as a comfortable base for the night.

Sightseeing: Triveni Sangam, Bade Hanuman Ji, Anand Bhavan, Alopi Devi and/or Khusro Bagh according to opening and interest

Overnight: Prayagraj

DAY 04

Prayagraj → Ayodhya — Ram Ki Paidi & Saryu

After breakfast, drive to Ayodhya and check into your hotel. Begin with the riverfront side of the city, which is a gentler introduction than entering the busiest temple precinct immediately after the road journey. Visit Nageshwarnath Temple and Ram Ki Paidi, then continue to the Saryu Aarti in the evening, using local transport where restricted vehicle access makes it necessary.

Sightseeing: Nageshwarnath Temple, Ram Ki Paidi, Saryu riverfront and evening Saryu Aarti

Overnight: Ayodhya

DAY 05

Ram Janmabhoomi & Old Ayodhya

Start relatively early for the principal temple area. Your guide will coordinate walking and local e-rickshaw movement because private vehicles cannot reach every entrance, and the sightseeing sequence will remain flexible around security and crowd controls. Visit the main sacred sites before taking a break, with Guptar Ghat or additional old-city time added later only if it suits your interests and energy.

Sightseeing: Shri Ram Janmabhoomi Mandir, Hanuman Garhi, Kanak Bhawan, Dashrath Mahal and optional Guptar Ghat

Overnight: Ayodhya

DAY 06

Departure from Ayodhya

Enjoy breakfast at the hotel and keep the final morning relaxed. Your private vehicle will transfer you to Ayodhya Airport or railway station according to your onward schedule. For a late departure, Nandigram and Bharat Kund can be added if there is enough time to visit them properly without compromising the transfer.

Sightseeing: Optional Nandigram and Bharat Kund for late departures

Overnight: Departure from Ayodhya

Practical questions

Questions travellers often ask

How is the pace of this journey?

Moderate. It combines three cities but allows time for the main experiences without trying to see every possible site. Private touring gives us room to make that adjustment properly, so a day does not have to be completed exactly as it appears on the website. We usually decide which experiences are essential for you, then remove or move the less important stops rather than compressing everything into a tiring schedule.

Is Prayagraj better as a day trip or overnight?

For this route, an overnight makes the Sangam and onward journey more comfortable and avoids an unnecessarily long day. The Sangam is best enjoyed when we are not watching the clock for a long onward drive, especially if there are traffic diversions or busy bathing dates. An overnight also gives us flexibility to choose a better time for the boat and to leave for the next city after breakfast rather than after a very long sightseeing day.

How much walking is involved in Ayodhya?

Moderate. Temple-area vehicle restrictions mean some walking and local transport are normally required. In practice, the amount of walking depends heavily on that day’s security cordons and where vehicles are stopped, so we never promise a fixed door-to-door drop. If you tell us about knee, back or mobility concerns in advance, we can reduce optional stops, allow more rest time and use approved local transport wherever it is actually permitted.

Can the programme be slowed down?

Yes. We can shorten individual sightseeing periods or add an extra night if you prefer a gentler pace. Private touring gives us room to make that adjustment properly, so a day does not have to be completed exactly as it appears on the website. We usually decide which experiences are essential for you, then remove or move the less important stops rather than compressing everything into a tiring schedule.

Are boat rides guaranteed?

No. Boat operations in Varanasi and at the Sangam remain subject to river and local administration conditions. This is one of the few parts of a Varanasi programme that no responsible operator should guarantee regardless of conditions, because the river is controlled by local safety orders. When boats are stopped, we do not waste the morning waiting at the ghat; the guide switches to a riverfront walk, viewpoints or another suitable experience.

Is this suitable for a first visit to North India?

Yes, especially for travellers interested in living Hindu traditions and who prefer private guiding and local support. The real question is how much pace you are comfortable with: a shorter stay can cover the essentials, but it leaves less room for hotel breaks, traffic delays and spontaneous local experiences. For first-time international travellers we usually prefer some breathing space between the early-morning river outing and the evening programme.
