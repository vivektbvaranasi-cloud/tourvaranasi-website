Itinerary

Your day-by-day itinerary

DAY 01

Arrive in Varanasi — Old City & Ganga Aarti

Settle into your hotel before meeting your guide for a gentle first look at Banaras. Walk through the Northern Bazaar and selected old-city lanes, pausing where useful to understand neighbourhood life, small shrines, local food and traditional commerce. Continue towards the river in time for the evening Ganga Aarti, with a comfortable viewing arrangement chosen according to the day, crowd levels and your preference.

Sightseeing: Northern Bazaar, old-city lanes, Dashashwamedh Ghat and evening Ganga Aarti

Overnight: Varanasi

DAY 02

Sunrise, Sacred Varanasi & Sarnath

Begin on the Ganges before sunrise, when the riverfront is quieter and the long line of ghats is easiest to appreciate. Follow the boat ride with a short walk, then return to the hotel for breakfast and a break. Later explore selected sacred and cultural parts of the city before travelling to Sarnath for the Buddhist archaeological remains, Dhamek Stupa and Mulagandha Kuti Vihara.

Sightseeing: Private sunrise boat, ghat walk, Kashi Vishwanath where appropriate, Sankat Mochan, Tulsi Manas, BHU & New Vishwanath Temple, Bharat Mata Temple, Dhamek Stupa, Chaukhandi Stupa and Mulagandha Kuti Vihara

Overnight: Varanasi

DAY 03

A Deeper Side of Banaras

The third day is deliberately less rushed and can be shaped around what interests you most. Spend time with Banarasi textile traditions, explore local food with a guide, visit the southern ghats, or choose a village or cultural experience. Travellers interested in the city’s relationship with life and death can also take a sensitively guided walk that explains the traditions without treating cremation as a spectacle.

Sightseeing: Banarasi weaving or craft visit, food walk, southern ghats, Ramnagar when suitable, village experience or a sensitively guided Death & Rebirth walk

Overnight: Varanasi / departure

Practical questions

Questions travellers often ask

Is three days a better choice for a first visit?

For many international travellers, yes. Three days gives enough time to see the main sights while keeping the pace comfortable. The real question is how much pace you are comfortable with: a shorter stay can cover the essentials, but it leaves less room for hotel breaks, traffic delays and spontaneous local experiences. For first-time international travellers we usually prefer some breathing space between the early-morning river outing and the evening programme.

Does the tour include Sarnath?

Yes. Sarnath is normally included as a separate half-day visit so it does not feel rushed. From our experience, rushing Sarnath into a one-hour photo stop misses the point; the relationship between the stupa, ruins, temple and museum area needs some unhurried time. We usually plan the visit away from the hottest part of the day when possible and keep a little flexibility for traffic between Varanasi and Sarnath.

How early is the sunrise boat ride?

Start time changes with sunrise and season. It is usually the earliest outing of the programme. This is one of the few parts of a Varanasi programme that no responsible operator should guarantee regardless of conditions, because the river is controlled by local safety orders. When boats are stopped, we do not waste the morning waiting at the ghat; the guide switches to a riverfront walk, viewpoints or another suitable experience.

Can we return to the hotel for breakfast and rest?

Yes. We normally prefer a break after the early river experience rather than continuing sightseeing without pause. From our experience, this works best when we keep a little flexibility rather than treating the published itinerary as a rigid timetable. We reconfirm the practical details close to travel and the guide can adjust the sequence on the day for traffic, crowds, opening conditions and your comfort.

Can Kashi Vishwanath Temple be included?

Yes, subject to current temple access and security arrangements. Your guide will explain the practical procedure close to the visit. From our experience, this works best when we keep a little flexibility rather than treating the published itinerary as a rigid timetable. We reconfirm the practical details close to travel and the guide can adjust the sequence on the day for traffic, crowds, opening conditions and your comfort.

Can the programme be customised?

Yes. Food, weaving, music, village visits and additional walks can be added or removed according to your interests. Private touring gives us room to make that adjustment properly, so a day does not have to be completed exactly as it appears on the website. We usually decide which experiences are essential for you, then remove or move the less important stops rather than compressing everything into a tiring schedule.
