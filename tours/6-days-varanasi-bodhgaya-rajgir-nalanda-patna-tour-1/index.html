Itinerary

Your day-by-day itinerary

DAY 01

Arrive Varanasi — Old City & Ganga Aarti

After arrival, settle into your hotel and meet your guide for a relaxed introduction to Varanasi. Walk through selected old-city and bazaar lanes towards the river, allowing time to understand the neighbourhoods rather than simply passing through them. End the evening at the Ganga Aarti from a suitable viewing point, with the exact arrangement adapted to crowd levels and local access.

Sightseeing: Northern Bazaar, old-city lanes, Dashashwamedh Ghat and evening Ganga Aarti

Overnight: Varanasi

DAY 02

Sunrise Varanasi & Sarnath

Begin on the Ganges before sunrise and continue with a short ghat walk before returning for breakfast. Later visit selected sacred and cultural parts of Varanasi, then travel to Sarnath for the Buddhist archaeological landscape. Your guide will connect the site with the Buddha’s first sermon and the early development of the Buddhist community.

Sightseeing: Private sunrise boat, selected sacred Varanasi, Dhamek Stupa, Chaukhandi Stupa, archaeological ruins and Mulagandha Kuti Vihara

Overnight: Varanasi

DAY 03

Varanasi → Bodh Gaya

After breakfast, travel privately to Bodh Gaya with comfort stops on the way. Check into your hotel and keep the late afternoon easy after the drive. When timings allow, visit the Mahabodhi Temple in the evening for a quieter first introduction to the pilgrimage centre before the more detailed sightseeing planned for the following day.

Sightseeing: Private road transfer and optional evening Mahabodhi Temple visit

Overnight: Bodh Gaya

DAY 04

Bodh Gaya

Explore the Mahabodhi Temple complex and Bodhi Tree with enough time for quiet observation as well as explanation. Continue to the Great Buddha Statue and a carefully selected group of international monasteries, avoiding the temptation to rush through every available temple. Sujata Village or Dungeshwari may be added when it suits the traveller’s historical interest and preferred pace.

Sightseeing: Mahabodhi Temple, Bodhi Tree, Great Buddha Statue, selected international monasteries, optional Sujata Village or Dungeshwari

Overnight: Bodh Gaya

DAY 05

Bodh Gaya → Rajgir → Nalanda → Patna

Travel first to Rajgir, where Buddhist history is set among low hills and ancient pilgrimage sites, then continue to nearby Nalanda. Walk through the extensive ruins of the ancient monastic university and visit the museum when open before driving onward to Patna. It is a full but rewarding day, so the programme focuses on the principal sites rather than adding unnecessary detours.

Sightseeing: Rajgir, Vishwa Shanti Stupa / Vulture’s Peak as appropriate, Nalanda archaeological ruins, Nalanda Museum when open and onward drive to Patna

Overnight: Patna

DAY 06

Patna & Departure

Use the final morning according to your interests and departure time. Patna offers very different heritage experiences, from the Bihar Museum to Takht Sri Patna Sahib, but we prefer selecting one or two meaningful visits instead of squeezing in everything. Continue by private vehicle to Patna Airport or railway station with a comfortable margin before departure.

Sightseeing: Bihar Museum and/or Takht Sri Patna Sahib according to interest and timing

Overnight: Departure from Patna

Practical questions

Questions travellers often ask

How much walking is involved at Nalanda?

Nalanda is an open archaeological site with uneven areas, so moderate walking should be expected. The route can be shortened. The main consideration is not distance alone but the open archaeological ground, occasional uneven surfaces and the heat during the middle of the day. We normally keep the vehicle as close as local access allows, build in pauses and avoid adding unnecessary stops when someone prefers an easier day.

Is Rajgir worth more than a quick stop?

Yes. Rajgir has enough to justify proper time, particularly for Buddhist and historical interests. Rajgir is not just a transfer stop between Bodh Gaya and Nalanda; the hills, Vulture’s Peak area and Buddhist associations deserve time if that history matters to you. In practice, we decide the depth of the visit according to the day’s driving time and the guest’s walking comfort rather than trying to include every site.

Do you use local guides?

For normal private travel, good local English-speaking guides can be arranged in Bodh Gaya and Rajgir/Nalanda. For us, the value of a guide is not only in dates and history but in handling the practical side of a visit—where to enter, what is open, how much time to allow and when a route should be changed. We prefer guides who can read the guest’s interest and pace instead of delivering the same memorised commentary to everyone.

Can this journey suit mature travellers?

Yes, with sensible pacing. The main considerations are archaeological-site walking and road travel between cities. In practice, we judge this more by the surfaces, crowds and access points than by kilometres alone, because those are what usually make a sightseeing day tiring. If you let us know your comfort level before arrival, we can shorten walking sections, add breaks and choose more convenient drop-off or meeting points wherever local rules allow.

Is an overnight in Patna always necessary?

No. Depending on flights, trains and the onward route, Patna can sometimes be used mainly as a transport point. We use Patna according to the transport logic of the journey, not simply because an itinerary template says an overnight must be there. If your flight or train timing allows a comfortable onward connection, the night can sometimes be removed; if not, staying avoids an unnecessarily risky or exhausting travel day.
