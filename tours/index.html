Private journeys through Varanasi and beyond.

Start with Varanasi at your own pace, then continue to Ayodhya, Prayagraj or the Buddhist heartlands if you have more time. Every journey is privately arranged around how these places actually work on the ground.

A useful place to begin

Choose the pace first. The sightseeing follows naturally.

Varanasi is easier when the day is planned around the river, traffic, temple access and the old city rather than around a long list of places. That is why we prefer to start with how much time you have and how you like to travel.

Some guests want an early start and a full day. Others prefer to return to the hotel after breakfast, rest, and come out again later. Both can work well. The journey should fit you, not the other way around.

Not sure how many days you need?

A practical guide to time in Varanasi.

There is no single “correct” duration, but these are the patterns that work best for most first-time visitors.

1 day

Possible if time is limited, but expect a full day and an early start.

2 days

The best first introduction for most travellers: river, old city, temples and Sarnath without overloading one day.

3 days

More breathing room for craft, food, neighbourhood walks or a slower rhythm between outings.

4 days

Best for travellers who prefer unhurried mornings, deeper local experiences and time to adapt as the city changes.

A practical note before you choose

We would rather adjust the route than pretend every day works the same.

VaranasiRiver conditions, temple access and traffic can change the best order of sightseeing, sometimes on the same day.
AyodhyaVehicle access near the principal temple areas is restricted, so walking and local e-rickshaw movement need to be factored into the day.
Long routesChitrakoot, Bodh Gaya, Kushinagar and Lumbini involve real road time. We plan around that rather than quoting unrealistic sightseeing hours.
