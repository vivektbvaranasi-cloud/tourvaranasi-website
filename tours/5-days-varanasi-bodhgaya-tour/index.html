Itinerary

Your day-by-day itinerary

DAY 01

Arrive Varanasi — Old City & Ganga Aarti

After arrival, take time to settle before meeting your private guide for an introduction to the old city. Walk through selected bazaar lanes and neighbourhoods towards the river, with your guide explaining the everyday traditions that sit alongside the city’s sacred life. End the evening at the Ganga Aarti from a suitable viewing position and return to the hotel without adding unnecessary sightseeing after travel.

Sightseeing: Northern Bazaar, old-city lanes, Dashashwamedh Ghat and evening Ganga Aarti

Overnight: Varanasi

DAY 02

Sunrise Varanasi & Sarnath

Begin before sunrise with a private boat on the Ganges and a short ghat walk before returning for breakfast. Continue later with selected sacred and cultural Varanasi, then travel to Sarnath, where the Buddha delivered his first sermon. The visit focuses on the main archaeological and Buddhist sites with enough time for interpretation rather than moving quickly through a long list.

Sightseeing: Private sunrise boat, Kashi Vishwanath where appropriate, BHU & New Vishwanath Temple, Bharat Mata Temple, Dhamek Stupa, Chaukhandi Stupa and Mulagandha Kuti Vihara

Overnight: Varanasi

DAY 03

Varanasi → Bodh Gaya

After breakfast, leave Varanasi by private vehicle for Bodh Gaya. The journey is treated as a proper travel day, with comfort stops as required, rather than trying to force too much sightseeing into the road transfer. After check-in, visit the Mahabodhi Temple in the evening if arrival time and energy permit; the temple atmosphere after dark can be one of the most memorable moments of the stay.

Sightseeing: Private road journey to Bodh Gaya and optional evening visit to Mahabodhi Temple

Overnight: Bodh Gaya

DAY 04

Bodh Gaya — The Buddhist Heart of the Journey

Spend the day exploring Bodh Gaya at a thoughtful pace. Begin with the Mahabodhi Temple complex and Bodhi Tree, then continue to the Great Buddha Statue and a small selection of international monasteries rather than rushing through every temple. Travellers with a deeper historical interest can add Sujata Village or the Dungeshwari Cave Temples, subject to time and walking preference.

Sightseeing: Mahabodhi Temple, Bodhi Tree, Great Buddha Statue, selected international monasteries, optional Sujata Village and Dungeshwari Cave Temples

Overnight: Bodh Gaya

DAY 05

Departure from Bodh Gaya / Gaya

Keep the final morning easy, with time for breakfast and perhaps another quiet visit to the Mahabodhi Temple if your onward schedule allows. Your private vehicle will then transfer you to Gaya Airport or railway station. We prefer to protect enough time for the transfer rather than add last-minute sightseeing that can make the departure unnecessarily stressful.

Sightseeing: Optional final Mahabodhi Temple visit and private departure transfer

Overnight: Departure

Practical questions

Questions travellers often ask

How demanding is the journey between Varanasi and Bodh Gaya?

The sightseeing itself can be comfortable, but the inter-city journey takes time. We plan breaks and recommend the most practical transport available for your dates. The road sector is the part guests feel most, so we plan a comfortable vehicle, sensible comfort stops and a departure time that avoids making the arrival evening unnecessarily late. Road conditions and traffic can vary, which is why we prefer not to combine a long transfer with too much sightseeing on the same day.

Is Sarnath included?

Varanasi programmes can include Sarnath, and Bodh Gaya then continues the Buddhist story in Bihar. We normally give Sarnath its own half day rather than squeezing it between temple visits, because the site becomes much more meaningful when there is time to walk and listen to the history. Traffic between central Varanasi and Sarnath can also vary, so keeping a sensible buffer makes the programme feel relaxed.

How much walking is involved at Mahabodhi Temple?

Usually light to moderate walking, with security procedures and footwear rules around the temple complex. In practice, we judge this more by the surfaces, crowds and access points than by kilometres alone, because those are what usually make a sightseeing day tiring. If you let us know your comfort level before arrival, we can shorten walking sections, add breaks and choose more convenient drop-off or meeting points wherever local rules allow.

Do we need the same guide throughout?

Not necessarily. Good local English-speaking guides are available in Bodh Gaya, while an accompanying guide can add continuity for special-interest journeys. For us, the value of a guide is not only in dates and history but in handling the practical side of a visit—where to enter, what is open, how much time to allow and when a route should be changed. We prefer guides who can read the guest’s interest and pace instead of delivering the same memorised commentary to everyone.

Can the pace be slowed down?

Yes. Extra time in Bodh Gaya can be added if you want more time for meditation, monasteries or rest. Private touring gives us room to make that adjustment properly, so a day does not have to be completed exactly as it appears on the website. We usually decide which experiences are essential for you, then remove or move the less important stops rather than compressing everything into a tiring schedule.
