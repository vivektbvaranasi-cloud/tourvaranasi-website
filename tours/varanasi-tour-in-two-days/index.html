Itinerary

Your day-by-day itinerary

DAY 01

Arrive in Varanasi — Old City & Ganga Aarti

After arrival, take time to settle into your hotel before meeting your private guide. Begin with an easy introduction to the old city, walking through the Northern Bazaar and historic lanes where flower sellers, small shrines, food stalls and everyday Banaras sit side by side. Continue towards the river and end the evening at the Ganga Aarti from a pre-arranged viewing position, with the exact access planned around local conditions.

Sightseeing: Northern Bazaar, old-city lanes, Dashashwamedh Ghat and evening Ganga Aarti

Overnight: Varanasi

DAY 02

Sunrise River, Sacred Varanasi & Sarnath

Start before sunrise with a private boat on the Ganges and watch the ghats come to life with bathing, prayers and morning rituals. After a short ghat walk, return for breakfast and some rest before continuing with selected sacred and cultural sights. Later visit Sarnath, where the Buddha delivered his first sermon, with enough time for the main archaeological and Buddhist sites rather than treating it as a quick stop.

Sightseeing: Private sunrise boat, ghat walk, Kashi Vishwanath where appropriate, Sankat Mochan, Tulsi Manas, BHU & New Vishwanath Temple, Bharat Mata Temple, Dhamek Stupa, Chaukhandi Stupa and Mulagandha Kuti Vihara

Overnight: Varanasi / departure

Practical questions

Questions travellers often ask

Is two days enough for Varanasi?

Yes. Two days is a good introduction for many first-time visitors, especially when the programme is privately planned around arrival and departure times. The real question is how much pace you are comfortable with: a shorter stay can cover the essentials, but it leaves less room for hotel breaks, traffic delays and spontaneous local experiences. For first-time international travellers we usually prefer some breathing space between the early-morning river outing and the evening programme.

How much walking is involved?

Walking is moderate. The old city has narrow and uneven lanes, but routes can be shortened for guests who prefer a gentler pace. In practice, we judge this more by the surfaces, crowds and access points than by kilometres alone, because those are what usually make a sightseeing day tiring. If you let us know your comfort level before arrival, we can shorten walking sections, add breaks and choose more convenient drop-off or meeting points wherever local rules allow.

Can cars enter the old city of Varanasi?

Only in some areas. Many lanes around the ghats and temples are pedestrian, so part of the experience is on foot. In the heritage and temple areas, the practical stopping point can change with police barricades, crowd levels and local traffic orders, so the vehicle may not be exactly where an online map suggests. We coordinate the driver and guide so the guest knows where the walking section begins and where the vehicle will meet again afterwards.

Is it better to see Ganga Aarti from a seat or a boat?

Both are worthwhile. Reserved seating is often more comfortable and predictable, while a boat gives a wider river view when operations are permitted. For many first-time guests, reserved seating is easier because the view is stable and boarding is not involved; a boat is more atmospheric when river conditions and crowd control allow it. We normally recommend the option after considering the season, mobility, river level and how busy the evening is expected to be.

What if boats cannot operate?

Boat services can be suspended because of river conditions or local orders. We adjust the programme rather locally than making a fixed guarantee. This is one of the few parts of a Varanasi programme that no responsible operator should guarantee regardless of conditions, because the river is controlled by local safety orders. When boats are stopped, we do not waste the morning waiting at the ghat; the guide switches to a riverfront walk, viewpoints or another suitable experience.
