Dev Diwali in Varanasi is the evening when the Ganga riverfront fills with the glow of earthen lamps. From a boat, you see illuminated steps rising towards temples and old buildings; on the ghats, you hear bells and prayers while families gather beside the water. It is a remarkable time to visit Kashi, provided the journey is planned around the festival.

Our strongest recommendation is to **stay at a suitable ghat hotel and settle in at least one day before Dev Diwali**. Reserve your boat in advance, keep the festival afternoon free, and expect walking wherever vehicles cannot enter. A private car is useful, but it cannot take you through a police barricade.

This guide explains what happens during Dev Deepawali, how our usual evening programme works, where to stay, why prices rise, and what to expect from a private boat or Bajra booking. It also explains the practical limits that matter once you are on the ground.

## What Dev Diwali celebrates

Dev Diwali, also written Dev Deepawali or Dev Deepavali, means the Diwali of the gods. It is associated with Kartik Purnima, the full moon of the Hindu month of Kartik, roughly a fortnight after Diwali. Its Gregorian date changes each year, so confirm the locally announced celebration date before booking travel.

In Hindu tradition, the festival is connected with Lord Shiva’s victory over Tripurasura. Devotees also believe that the gods descend to bathe in the Ganga. Lighting diyas, offering prayers and bathing in the river express this faith. These traditions are described by [Incredible India, the Ministry of Tourism’s travel portal](https://www.incredibleindia.gov.in/en/festivals-and-events/uttar-pradesh/illuminating-the-eternal-spirit-of-kashi).

The distinction between Diwali and Dev Diwali matters when choosing dates. Visiting Varanasi for the main Diwali holiday does not automatically mean you will see the large Dev Deepawali illumination of the ghats. They are separate occasions, with separate travel arrangements.

## What happens throughout the day

![Lighting earthen lamps for Dev Deepawali](/assets/images/dev-diwali/lighting-diyas.webp)

*Lighting diyas is part of the festival’s shared religious observance.*

The religious observance begins well before the evening boat programme. Pilgrims come for Kartik Purnima bathing and prayers, while people prepare lamps along the riverfront. The festival belongs to the city’s residents and worshippers as much as to visitors.

As dusk approaches, rows of diyas are lit across the ghat steps and other sacred spaces. The changing light is part of the experience: buildings visible in daylight become silhouettes, the lines of lamps grow brighter, and reflections spread across the water. [Akashvani’s account of the festival](https://newsonair.gov.in/dev-deepawali-in-varanasi-marks-kartik-purnima-with-diyas-and-riverfront-festivities/) describes the lamps across ghats, temples and rooftops.

Ganga Aarti adds the sound of chanting, bells and conches to the evening. From a boat, you experience the wider riverfront and may watch the ceremony from an authorised position on the river. The view of individual priests depends on distance, other vessels and the position permitted that evening.

**Guests can watch the fireworks from the boat, and the sound-and-light or projection show at Chet Singh Ghat is another highlight of the river view.** From a suitably positioned boat, you can enjoy the illuminated facade and fireworks without moving through the crowds on the steps. [The 2025 celebration featured a 25-minute Kashi Katha projection at Chet Singh Ghat and synchronised green fireworks](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/varanasi/yogi-lights-first-diya-cruises-the-ganga-to-witness-festivity/articleshow/125116855.cms). The programme, show timings and permitted boat positions can change each year, so we confirm the arrangements for your evening rather than promise an uninterrupted view from every part of the river.

For Tour Varanasi guests, the Dev Diwali evening is normally planned from around **4:00 PM until approximately 8:30 PM**, with **roughly four hours aboard the boat**. Reporting, boarding and disembarkation account for the difference between the programme window and time on the water.

This is our usual planning framework. It is not a universal start and finish time for every religious observance, boat operator or public event.

## Why we recommend staying at a ghat hotel

**For a visit centred on Dev Diwali, a well-chosen hotel on the ghats is our first recommendation.** Being settled near the river before the busiest period gives you a much easier base for the evening.

You can eat, rest and prepare nearby, then follow the agreed route to your boarding point. After the boat ride, returning to a nearby hotel can reduce your dependence on roads that are still congested or restricted. A suitable terrace or river-facing space may also let you enjoy part of the atmosphere from the property.

Choose the exact hotel carefully. “Near the Ganges” can mean a riverfront building, a room in a narrow lane, or a property that still requires a considerable walk. A river-view room does not necessarily include access to a festival terrace or a clear view of the main Aarti.

Before confirming, ask about the route from the road to reception, steps, lift access, luggage assistance, meals and the distance to your boat’s boarding point. If the hotel offers a boat transfer, its availability and hours need to fit your arrival; a normal transfer service cannot be assumed to operate throughout the festival evening.

For guests with limited mobility, we check the physical approach before recommending any ghat hotel. A beautiful location is useful only when you can comfortably enter and leave it. Our [guide to where to stay in Varanasi](https://www.tourvaranasi.com/blogs/post/where-to-stay-in-varanasi/) explains the wider choice of hotel areas.

## Arrive at least one day before the festival

We strongly advise arriving and settling into your hotel **at least a day before Dev Diwali**. This gives you time to recover from travel, understand your surroundings and discuss the next day’s meeting arrangements without rushing.

It also allows us to place other priorities, such as Kashi Vishwanath darshan or a regular evening Aarti visit, outside the main festival programme. You should not need to choose between reaching your boat and finishing an overfilled sightseeing list.

If you must arrive on the festival day, choose an early arrival and allow a generous margin to reach the hotel. Morning arrival is preferable to an afternoon connection. Flight or train arrival time is only the beginning: baggage collection, the road transfer, diversions and the final approach to a ghat hotel all take time.

An early arrival does not automatically provide an early room check-in. Confirm whether the hotel can receive your luggage and provide somewhere to wait if the room is not ready. Keep the rest of that day simple and be prepared before the agreed reporting time.

## Vehicle restrictions and the walk to the ghats

At many ghat approaches, guests should plan for **around one kilometre or more of walking between the designated vehicle parking or stopping point and the riverfront**. This is a working estimate, not a fixed maximum. Barricades, crowds and the chosen boarding ghat can increase the distance; vehicles may also be held before their usual parking area.

The return is just as important as the approach. **Guests must walk back to the permitted vehicle meeting point after the programme.** A driver cannot collect you from the ghat if the road remains closed. Having a private vehicle waiting does not remove this walking section.

Where guiding or escort assistance forms part of your booking, the representative can help you follow the agreed route and locate the vehicle. Guests still need to cover the distance on foot unless a specific, permitted alternative has been arranged.

Confirm the meeting point before boarding. Save your driver’s number, the vehicle details and a recognisable landmark. Keep your group together after disembarkation, and follow instructions if a different exit is opened.

Published traffic plans demonstrate how extensive these controls can be. [Dainik Jagran’s report of the 2025 plan](https://www.jagran.com/travel/miscellaneous-varanasi-dev-deepawali-2025-traffic-plan-route-chart-and-parking-40027712.html) described multiple diversions and separate parking arrangements. The applicable plan must be checked for each festival.

## Why we do not recommend Tempo Travellers or coaches for the evening

**We do not recommend Tempo Travellers or coaches for transfers to the Dev Diwali ghats.** These larger vehicles are commonly stopped much farther away, creating longer walks and a more difficult return for the group.

A coach can work for other parts of a journey, including intercity travel. The festival evening needs a separate movement plan. Where appropriate, we recommend smaller cars for the permitted road sections and organise the group around clear meeting points.

Smaller vehicles do not have unrestricted access either. Their advantage is practical flexibility where movement is allowed; they cannot cross a closure simply because they carry hotel guests.

For groups, the transport plan should be agreed before confirming the programme. Consider the slowest walkers, the size of the party and the time needed to assemble everyone after the boat ride. A single departure instruction for a large group is not enough if people are returning through different lanes.

## How visits by the Chief Minister or Prime Minister can affect access

A visit by the Chief Minister, Prime Minister or another protected dignitary can lead to additional diversions, temporary road closures and restrictions around particular ghats. It may also affect the timing or route of permitted river movement.

This does not mean such a visit takes place every year. It means that the plan must remain flexible if one is announced.

Your original drop-off point, boarding approach or return route may need to change. Follow the local team’s revised instructions even if a navigation app continues to show the usual road.

No hotel reservation, private boat booking or guide arrangement overrides a security restriction. For this reason, we avoid promising unrestricted access to a particular ghat or an unchangeable sailing route. [Reporting from the 2025 festival](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/varanasi/yogi-lights-first-diya-cruises-the-ganga-to-witness-festivity/articleshow/125116855.cms) described controlled traffic and designated river lanes alongside the Chief Minister’s visit.

## Why we recommend a double decker Bajra for Dev Diwali

**For Dev Diwali evening, Tour Varanasi strongly recommends a properly operated double-decker Bajra.** Its raised viewing deck offers a broader perspective of the illuminated ghats, fireworks and the Chet Singh Ghat sound-and-light show when the vessel is in a suitable authorised position. The additional deck space can also make the evening more comfortable for your group.

We do not recommend choosing a small boat simply to save money on this exceptionally busy evening. Small, low-sided boats can be vulnerable to the wakes of cruises and other large vessels. In congested water, a collision or water entering the boat can create serious risks, including capsizing or sinking. For this festival, our preference is a suitable Bajra with an experienced crew and properly managed passenger numbers.

A **Bajra** is a larger traditional riverboat, but layouts and facilities vary. When booking, specifically request the **double-decker arrangement** and confirm upper-deck access, railings, steps, seating and permitted occupancy. The name alone does not confirm a toilet, catering or a particular layout.

Safety depends on the individual vessel, its condition, crew, loading and operation; two decks alone do not make a boat safer. We recommend a licensed, well-maintained vessel with life jackets for every guest, including correctly sized jackets for children. Follow the crew’s seating and movement instructions, and avoid crowding one side or the upper deck. Your safety takes priority over getting closer to a show or securing a photograph.

For any vessel, ask how the return works if someone becomes uncomfortable. Do not assume that immediate disembarkation at the nearest ghat will always be possible.

## Why hotel and boat prices rise so sharply

Dev Diwali concentrates demand for rooms, river views and boats into a short period. A normal sightseeing budget is therefore a poor guide to the cost of the festival visit.

From our local operating experience, **hotel quotations during the peak festival dates can approach five to six times the property’s usual rate**. Boat and Bajra charges can be **more than ten times ordinary-day charges**, particularly for sought-after private arrangements.

These are possible festival premiums, not a fixed multiplier applied by every supplier. The hotel, room category, booking date, vessel and inclusions all matter. A four-hour festival allocation also differs from a short ordinary-day river trip.

Historical reporting confirms that sharp increases are a recurring feature. [The Times of India’s report on a previous Dev Deepawali](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/varanasi/1l-per-day-kashi-hotel-tariffs-hit-the-roof-on-dev-deepawali/articleshow/95368921.cms) documented large hotel premiums and boat prices many times normal rates. Those historical rupee figures should not be used as a current quotation.

Request a written total showing taxes, meals, transfers, guiding and boat arrangements separately. Ask about any hotel minimum stay, payment dates and cancellation conditions before confirming. We provide the special rate applicable to our available reserved boat or Bajra inventory for your party.

## What to confirm for four hours aboard

Comfort matters during a long evening on the water. Before selecting a vessel, confirm the type of seating, whether the deck is open or covered, and whether a toilet is available. Many smaller boats do not have one; never assume that a large or decorated boat includes every facility.

Have a meal and use a washroom before reporting. Carry drinking water and check whether refreshments are included. If you require food at a particular time, discuss it in advance rather than relying on being able to buy something after departure.

Bring a light layer for the river breeze, comfortable footwear for steps and a charged phone. Keep baggage compact so boarding and seating remain manageable. Carry any personal essentials you need for the whole programme.

Wear the life jacket provided and follow the crew’s instructions, including when taking photographs or moving between seats. Passenger limits must be respected. For children, request correctly sized life jackets when booking. These precautions are consistent with the safety measures described in [reporting on the 2025 festival](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/india/dev-deepawali-2025-tourism-trade-and-faith-boost-varanasi-s-economy-13652365.html).

## Planning for older guests and families

Dev Diwali can be rewarding for older travellers, but the plan must reflect their actual walking, standing and boarding comfort. Tell us about difficulty with steps, balance, uneven surfaces or long periods away from a washroom.

A ghat hotel may reduce the evening’s road travel, yet its approach can still involve stairs. A Bajra may offer more space, but reaching its deck may require steps. Neither choice automatically makes the visit accessible.

For a guest who cannot manage the approach to a boat, an appropriate hotel terrace may be a better way to enjoy the illumination. Confirm the view, access, seating and any separate charge before booking.

For families, keep children close during the approach and return walk. Set a meeting plan, travel with essential snacks where permitted, and allow time for an unhurried dinner afterwards. We prefer a comfortable, realistic experience over adding activities that leave the group exhausted before the lamps are lit.

## What you should and should not expect

Expect a shared city celebration, crowds, waiting and some walking. Even a carefully organised private programme takes place within a busy public event.

Do not expect all lamps to be lit at one exact minute or the riverfront to look identical from every position. Your view changes with the route, distance and surrounding vessels.

Do not assume a boat will cover every named ghat, stop directly in front of the main priests or make repeated landings. The permitted route and river traffic determine movement.

Do not assume fireworks, a drone show or a projection display will take place every year at a fixed time. Only the programme announced for your visit can establish those details.

Do not expect a terrace seat, meal, toilet, private guide or transfer simply because a booking is described as “premium”. The written inclusions should identify each service.

A boat ticket does not include temple entry or priority darshan unless separately specified. Likewise, a hotel’s ordinary complimentary boat transfer is not automatically a Dev Diwali sightseeing cruise.

Do not count on an app-based cab, e-rickshaw or coach collecting you beside the ghats after the event. Plan the return walk and permitted pickup before the evening begins.

Photography should respect worshippers and local instructions. Avoid obstructing steps or moving dangerously for a better view. Do not fly a personal drone without the permissions applicable to the location and date.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I watch Dev Diwali without booking a boat?**

Yes. Depending on permitted access, you can watch from the ghats or a suitable terrace. Public viewing does not automatically provide reserved seating, personal space or an easy exit. Choose the arrangement that suits your mobility and the experience you want.

**Is a ghat hotel the best choice?**

For a trip focused on Dev Diwali, it is our preferred choice when its access suits the guest. Staying near the river can make the evening easier, particularly after disembarkation. Check the specific property and boarding route before deciding.

**Can I arrive on the same day?**

Yes, but arrive early enough to reach your hotel and prepare well before reporting. We strongly prefer arrival at least one day earlier. An afternoon arrival leaves little room for delays or restrictions.

**Is the boat ride exactly four hours?**

We normally plan around four hours aboard, within an evening programme of approximately 4:00–8:30 PM. The confirmed reporting time and actual river movement govern the experience. Hotel transfer time is additional.

**How far will I have to walk?**

Plan for around one kilometre or more at many ghat approaches, each way between the riverfront and the permitted vehicle point. Your exact distance depends on the hotel, boarding point, vehicle and restrictions.

**Can our Tempo Traveller take us to the boat?**

We do not recommend it for the festival evening. Larger vehicles are stopped farther away. A separate plan using smaller vehicles where allowed, followed by the necessary walk, is usually more practical.

**Does a confirmed boat mean sailing is guaranteed whatever happens?**

Your confirmed allocation is guaranteed under your booking. Operation still follows official permission, river conditions and safety instructions. Your confirmation should explain the terms applicable if an authority changes or stops operations.
