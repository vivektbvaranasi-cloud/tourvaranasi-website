## Day 2 — Imambaras Rumi Darwaza art gallery and historic Lucknow

### Bara Imambara with a private guide

After breakfast, begin your exploration of old Lucknow with [Bara Imambara](https://nri.up.gov.in/article/en/bara-imambara). The scale of its central hall makes a strong first impression, but the visit becomes more rewarding when your guide explains its religious purpose and the patronage behind its construction.

An imambara is a place associated with Shia religious observance. Understanding that context helps you appreciate the complex respectfully as well as architecturally.

If you are comfortable with stairs and enclosed passages, the Bhul Bhulaiya can form part of the visit with the required authorised guidance. It is optional for guests who prefer to avoid climbing or confined spaces. We agree on a practical route for the whole party, including anyone who would like to remain in the more accessible areas.

Your guide will explain the etiquette and access arrangements for the sections you visit.

### Chhota Imambara and Rumi Darwaza

Continue to [Chhota Imambara](https://lucknow.nic.in/tourist-place/chhota-imam-bara/), where the experience shifts towards detail: the courtyard, decorative surfaces, mirrors and chandeliers. Your guide connects these features with the building’s religious purpose and its place in Lucknow’s courtly history.

Next, visit **Rumi Darwaza**, one of the city’s most recognisable gateways. Take time to appreciate the proportions and decoration, with a stop for photographs where it is safe and permitted.

![Visitors at Rumi Darwaza in Lucknow](/assets/images/regional-luxury/lucknow-rumi-darwaza.webp)

### Hussainabad Picture Gallery

Continue with an **art gallery visit at Hussainabad Picture Gallery**. Your guide introduces the works on display and relates them to the city’s Nawabi past, adding a visual perspective to the architecture you have explored during the morning.

The sequence of the morning visits is coordinated around opening arrangements and local traffic, with walking distances adjusted to your party.

### Lunch break

Pause for lunch before the afternoon sightseeing. This interval allows time to sit down and refresh after the morning visits. A hotel rest can be built in where practical within the day’s route and opening hours.

Lunch can be taken at your hotel or a pre-agreed restaurant according to your preference. It is quoted separately unless included in your booked meal plan.

### Afternoon visit to the British Residency

In the afternoon, explore the [British Residency](https://lucknow.nic.in/tourist-place/residency/) with your guide. The surviving buildings, ruins and grounds introduce another chapter of Lucknow’s history, particularly the events of 1857.

Walk through selected parts of the complex while your guide explains how the site developed and why it became central to the conflict. The visit allows time to look closely at the remaining structures and understand their relationship to the wider city. The museum may be included when open and time permits.

![Historic ruins and grounds at the Lucknow Residency](/assets/images/regional-luxury/lucknow-residency.webp)

### Photo stop at Dilkusha Kothi

Continue to [Dilkusha Kothi](https://lucknow.nic.in/tourist-place/dilkusha-palace/) for a **photo stop**. The remains of this former royal retreat and the surrounding greenery provide a different architectural setting from the Imambaras visited earlier.

Your guide offers a brief introduction, with time for photographs from the permitted viewing areas. This is planned as a short stop before returning to the hotel, keeping the afternoon manageable.

The evening is free to relax. If you have chosen an optional dinner with Nawab Saheb, we coordinate that separately around the confirmed appointment.

Return to the hotel for your second overnight stay.
